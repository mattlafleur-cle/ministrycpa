Clergy Taxes

The clergy housing allowance, explained

By Matt LaFleur, CPA

In short: a minister's housing allowance can be excluded from federal income tax, but not from self-employment (SECA) tax, and only if the church designates it in advance and the minister stays within three limits.

Who qualifies

A licensed, commissioned, or ordained minister performing ministerial services as an employee may exclude a church-designated housing allowance, or the fair rental value of a parsonage, from gross income (IRS Topic 417).

Step 1: Designate it in advance

The church must officially designate the housing allowance before paying it, usually by board or council resolution. A designation made after the fact, even informally agreed to, does not count. If your current resolution doesn't have a clear dollar amount, or nobody remembers the last time it was reviewed, that's worth fixing before the next pay period, not at tax time.

Step 2: The three limits

The minister may exclude the lowest of three amounts:

  1. The amount officially designated by the church
  2. The amount actually spent to provide or rent a home
  3. The fair market rental value of the home, furnished, including utilities

The exclusion also can't exceed reasonable compensation overall. Any excess is taxable wages (IRS, Ministers' Compensation & Housing Allowance FAQ).

Step 3: Remember SECA

The housing allowance and parsonage value are excluded only for income tax. They're still included in net earnings for self-employment tax on Schedule SE (IRS Schedule SE instructions). This is the single most common surprise for pastors at tax time.

Homeowners

A minister who owns a home may still deduct mortgage interest and real estate taxes if itemizing, even though the housing allowance also covers those same costs (IRS Topic 417).

What to keep on record

The church should keep the dated resolution, the designated amount, and W-2 reporting that matches it. The minister should keep records of actual housing costs and support for fair rental value, in case the exclusion is ever questioned.

Common mistakes

  • Designating the allowance after the year, or the pay period, has already started
  • Designating an amount far below (or suspiciously close to 100% of) actual compensation, with no documentation to back it up
  • Forgetting SECA, and being surprised by the Schedule SE bill
  • Treating the pastor as a 1099 contractor instead of a common-law employee
  • Withholding FICA from a minister's pay, when ministers pay SECA instead

Quick answers

Is a minister's housing allowance taxable? It can be excluded from federal income tax if the church designates it in advance, in writing, and it stays within the limits above. It's still included in earnings for self-employment (SECA) tax.

Do churches withhold Social Security and Medicare from a minister's pay? No. Ministers are self-employed for Social Security and Medicare purposes and pay SECA directly on Schedule SE. A church that withholds FICA from a minister's paycheck is making a common, correctable mistake.
