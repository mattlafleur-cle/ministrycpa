The Housing Allowance Mistake 80% of Churches Make

By Matt LaFleur, CPA

Most churches treat the housing allowance like a form you fill out once. A board votes on a number, it goes in the pastor's file, and nobody looks at it again until someone asks about it at tax time. That habit, more than any single number, is the mistake I see most often. The IRS does not care how reasonable your intentions were. It cares about timing and paperwork.

The rule almost nobody applies correctly

To exclude a housing allowance from federal income tax, a minister's employing church has to officially designate the allowance in advance and in writing, before the compensation is paid out. Not at year-end. Not when the accountant is putting together the pastor's return. Before the year, or the pay period, begins. An informal understanding that "some of his pay is for housing" does not count, and neither does a designation your board only gets around to approving in March for the year that already started in January.

If the designation happens after the fact, the exclusion is gone for that period. There is no way to fix it retroactively. That is the part that catches treasurers off guard: this is one of the few areas of church finance where good intentions and a reasonable number do not save you if the process was backwards.

The cap most churches forget entirely

Even when the paperwork is done correctly, the amount a pastor can actually exclude is not just whatever the board designated. It is the smallest of three numbers:

  • The amount the church officially designated as housing allowance
  • What the pastor actually spent on housing that year (rent or mortgage, utilities, furnishings, repairs, and similar costs)
  • The fair rental value of the home, furnished, plus utilities

A church that designates $30,000 as housing allowance for a pastor whose actual housing costs come to $22,000 has not created a $30,000 exclusion. The extra $8,000 is taxable income, whether or not anyone reported it that way. Most churches never check this cap against actual spending. Most pastors do not track their housing costs closely enough to know if they are under it or over it.

The self-employment tax surprise

Housing allowance is excluded from federal income tax, but it is not excluded from self-employment tax. Ministers pay SECA on their ministerial earnings, and that calculation includes the housing allowance (or the fair rental value of a parsonage) even though it never shows up as taxable wages. Pastors who only think about the income tax side are routinely surprised by how much they owe on Schedule SE.

What to actually do about it

This is fixable, and it takes one board meeting, not a policy overhaul:

  • Pass a written housing allowance resolution before the year starts, or before a new pastor's first paycheck, specifying a dollar amount, not a percentage or a vague description
  • Review it at least once a year. Compensation changes, housing costs change, and a designation from three years ago may no longer reflect either
  • Ask the pastor to keep a simple running record of actual housing expenses, so there is something to check the designation against if the return is ever questioned
The number matters less than the order of operations. Designate first, pay second. That single habit prevents most of the housing allowance problems I see.

If your church's current housing allowance resolution does not have a clear dollar amount, or nobody remembers the last time it was reviewed, that is worth fixing this month, not at tax time next year.

— Matt LaFleur, CPA
